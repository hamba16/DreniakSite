# Photography review — 21 September 2026

## Scope and decisions

Reviewed both Engineering and Asset Management, the parent homepage, sector pages, Projects, Insights, Approach, shared components, leadership and supplied project imagery. The original list contains 53 candidates; all file pages and previews were inspected. Six originals were selected, decoded and exported locally. No external hotlinks are required to display them.

The former generated library comprises 16 visual-depth files plus two original heroes and two natural-colour approach variants. All 20 remain on disk for traceability, but none is referenced by the public image renderer or hero/approach components. Eight sector slots use reviewed photographs. Government, healthcare, education, geotechnical, project management, intervention and the two editorial slots have no adequately matched candidate in this list and render their existing text/icon treatment. A generic building is not substituted for a hospital, campus or government portfolio.

The parent and division Engineering images use the Nairobi Expressway. Asset Management uses the Tanger Med port aerial, showing connected infrastructure and logistics. Actual locations, photographers, capture years and licences are visible through captions and /image-credits. These are African sector examples, not Dreniak work or Ugandan sites. The 2009 Nairobi photograph is explicitly dated.

The shared sector component previously assigned the latest CMS uploads by array index to unrelated sector positions. That positional override is removed from the sector display so only explicitly reviewed mappings render. The CMS upload system and database are unchanged. Future sector replacements must be reviewed and mapped explicitly.

Leadership portraits are real-person source photographs with previously AI-edited backgrounds; retained, with disclosure on the credits page. The supplied construction-progress photograph, AG Rosa architectural rendering and founder image are retained. Their provenance is based on repository records, not an independent forensic authenticity assessment. Unused portrait cutout derivatives also remain untouched.

## Reuse and provenance

The selected file pages identify five photographs under CC BY-SA 4.0 and the Uhuru Park photograph under CC BY 2.0. Each placement links its source and licence. The public credits page explains resizing, WebP conversion, responsive crops and hero overlays, and provides the derivatives under the respective source licence. Daniel Case's photograph is used under its offered CC BY-SA option. No photographer endorsement is implied.

The Kigali candidates were excluded because architectural reuse rights were not established; see [Commons Rwanda guidance](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Rwanda#Freedom_of_panorama). Kenyan public-view architecture is covered in [Commons Kenya guidance](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Kenya#Freedom_of_panorama). These checks record the selection basis, not a blanket guarantee about all third-party rights.

Machine-readable review: IMAGE_CANDIDATE_REVIEW.json. Selected original/derivative SHA-256 hashes and source URLs: PHOTOGRAPHY_PROVENANCE.json. Runtime attribution and mapping: src/content/photography.ts. Existing conceptual component names remain to avoid unrelated CSS/API churn; they now render approved photographs only.
